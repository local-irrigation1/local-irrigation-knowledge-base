## Uneven water distribution in Clearwater

Uneven water distribution, called inconsistent watering, occurs when an irrigation system delivers different volumes of water across a coverage area. Some zones become saturated. Others stay dry. The result is patchy grass, stressed plants, wasted water, and accelerating turf decline across residential and commercial landscapes in Clearwater, FL. Uneven water distribution is classified as a Water Distribution Issue, meaning the root problem lies in how water moves through the system rather than in the soil or plant itself. Irregular watering of this kind is one of the most common reasons lawns develop brown patches, fungal growth, and compaction problems even when the irrigation timer runs on schedule. ### Scope and component parts of uneven water distribution

Uneven water distribution spans three core components: zone coverage, spray pattern, and flow rate. Zone coverage refers to the geographic area each irrigation zone is designed to reach. When head spacing is wrong or a head sinks below grade, coverage gaps open immediately. Spray pattern is the arc and radius each head throws. A clogged nozzle or bent riser collapses that pattern to a fraction of its design radius. Flow rate, measured in gallons per minute, sets how much water each zone receives per cycle, and a single broken lateral line can drop flow to downstream heads by a measurable margin. All three components must work together for a zone to deliver water evenly.

### Measurable attributes: how to quantify uneven coverage

Uneven water distribution is measurable across five attributes that give technicians objective data for diagnosing the condition. The attributes are listed below.

- **Distribution Uniformity (DU):** Distribution Uniformity is a 0-to-100 percent score that compares the lowest-quarter average catch-can reading to the average. A well-tuned residential system typically scores 70 percent or higher; a system with significant irregular watering often scores below 50 percent.

- **Flow rate variance:** Flow rate variance is the difference in gallons per minute between the highest-output head and the lowest-output head in the same zone. A variance greater than 0.5 gpm across matched heads signals a blockage, leak or pressure problem.

- **Pressure differential:** Pressure differential is the psi difference between the supply point and the farthest head in a zone. A differential above 10 psi typically causes the near heads to mist and the far heads to underperform, producing uneven coverage across the zone. [Low sprinkler water pressure](https://local-irrigation.mintlify.site/low-sprinkler-water-pressure) is one of the most direct causes of a high pressure differential.

- **Coverage area discrepancy:** Coverage area discrepancy is the square footage difference between the designed throw radius and the actual wetted area measured on the ground. A head rated for a 15-foot radius that only wets 9 feet leaves 36 percent of its intended area dry.

- **Precipitation Rate Variability:** Precipitation Rate Variability is the percentage difference in inches-per-hour output between heads in the same zone. Mixing rotary heads with fixed spray heads in one zone is a common cause, since rotary heads typically apply 0.4 to 1.0 inches per hour while fixed sprays apply 1.3 to 2.0 inches per hour.

### How uneven water distribution relates to the broader sprinkler system

Uneven water distribution affects the sprinkler system as a whole because every zone shares the same supply line, controller and backflow preventer. A pressure drop caused by a cracked lateral in Zone 2 reduces available pressure in Zone 3 and Zone 4 during simultaneous operation, spreading the coverage problem beyond the zone where the break occurred. The condition requires sprinkler repair to resolve, since adjusting a controller schedule cannot compensate for a physical defect in a head, nozzle or pipe. Diagnosing uneven water distribution correctly means tracing the problem from the controller output through the valve, the lateral lines and finally the heads, rather than treating each zone in isolation. ### Common signs of irregular watering in Clearwater landscapes

Irregular watering produces visible patterns in turf and plant beds that distinguish it from drought stress or disease. The signs are listed below.

- **Dry arcs between heads:** Dry arcs between heads appear when head spacing exceeds the throw radius, leaving a gap that no head reaches. These arcs follow the geometry of the head layout and stay consistent across every watering cycle.

- **Soggy low spots:** Soggy low spots form directly in front of a head that is over-pressured or tilted, concentrating output in one small area while the rest of the zone stays dry.

- **Patchy turf color:** Patchy turf color, alternating between dark green and yellow-brown, is the most visible sign of uneven coverage and often the first thing a homeowner notices before any mechanical problem is apparent.

- **Fungal growth:** Fungal growth, including gray leaf spot and brown patch, develops in zones that receive too much water because the soil stays saturated between cycles, creating the humidity conditions fungus needs.

- **Runoff on dry areas:** Runoff on dry areas happens when a high-precipitation-rate head applies water faster than the soil can absorb it, sending water off the lawn while an adjacent dry patch receives nothing.

### Fixing uneven water distribution: diagnosis and repair approach

Fixing uneven water distribution starts with a catch-can test. Small containers are placed across a zone, the system runs for a set time, and technicians measure how much water each container collects. The difference between the highest and lowest readings directly shows the Distribution Uniformity score and pinpoints which areas of the zone are under- or over-watered. After the test, the repair sequence follows the data: nozzles are cleaned or replaced, heads are adjusted to the correct arc and radius, pressure-regulating stems are installed where pressure differential is high, and broken laterals are repaired to restore full flow rate. Address uneven distribution problems in Clearwater landscapes before the dry season. Florida's sandy soils lose moisture quickly, and a low-DU system accelerates turf stress within days of a missed watering. ### Uneven water distribution versus even water distribution

Uneven water distribution is the opposite of even water distribution, where every point in a zone receives the same precipitation rate within an acceptable tolerance. Uniform water distribution, the target condition, produces a Distribution Uniformity score above 70 percent, consistent turf color across the zone and no dry arcs or soggy spots between cycles. Efficient irrigation, the broader goal, depends on uniform coverage because a system that wastes water on saturated areas while leaving dry patches forces homeowners to run longer cycles to compensate, increasing water use without improving turf health. The gap between uneven and uniform coverage is almost always mechanical, meaning [sprinkler pressure and flow problems](https://local-irrigation.mintlify.site/low-sprinkler-water-pressure) are the first place to look when a zone fails to reach uniform output.
