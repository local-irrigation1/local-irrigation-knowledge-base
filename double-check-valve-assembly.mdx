## Double check valve assembly in Clearwater

A double check valve assembly, called a double check valve or double check assembly, is a type of backflow preventer consisting of two independent spring-loaded check valves arranged in series inside a single valve body, with test cocks and shutoff valves at each end, designed to stop contaminated water from flowing backward into a potable water supply. It is a backflow preventer, placing it in the same broader category as reduced pressure zone assemblies, pressure vacuum breakers and air gaps. ### Parts of a double check valve assembly

Six core components form a double check valve assembly: a valve body, two check valves, two shutoff valves and a set of test cocks. The valve body is most commonly cast from brass or bronze, though stainless steel bodies are used where corrosion resistance is a priority. Each check valve sits on a spring that holds it closed until forward flow pressure overcomes the spring tension, then snaps it shut the moment pressure reverses. The test cocks are small ports threaded into the body between and outside the two check valves; a certified tester connects differential pressure gauges to those ports during [double check valve assembly testing](https://local-irrigation.mintlify.site/lawn-sprinkler-system-contractor) to verify each check valve holds independently. Shutoff valves at the inlet and outlet let a technician isolate the assembly without draining the whole system. Double check valve assemblies carry several rated attributes that determine whether a given unit fits a specific application. Working pressure for most assemblies is rated between 150 and 175 psi, which covers the operating range of residential and light commercial water service. The Cv rating, a measure of flow capacity in gallons per minute at one psi of pressure drop, typically falls between 5 and 10 for assemblies sized for irrigation lines. Pressure loss across the assembly, measured in psi, depends on flow rate and connection size; higher flow rates push pressure loss toward the upper end of the rated range. Connection size is stated in inches and must match the pipe diameter at the point of installation. Body material, whether brass, bronze or stainless steel, affects both pressure rating and service life in corrosive soil or water conditions. A [sprinkler double check valve](https://local-irrigation.mintlify.site/pop-up-sprinkler) sized for a residential zone is typically a smaller connection size than one protecting a commercial main, so matching the Cv rating to the system's peak demand flow is the first step in selecting the right assembly. Many irrigation systems benefit from [pressure regulating valve](https://local-irrigation.mintlify.site/pressure-regulating-valve) installation downstream of the assembly to maintain stable downstream pressure.

### How a double check valve assembly fits into a backflow prevention system

A double check valve assembly serves as one device in a layered backflow prevention strategy rather than a standalone solution for every hazard level. Backflow prevention devices are classified by the degree of hazard they protect against, and the double check assembly is rated for low-to-moderate hazard applications where the risk is contamination rather than a toxic or health-threatening substance. For high-hazard connections, a reduced pressure zone assembly (RPZ assembly) is required instead; the RPZ adds a relief valve between the two check valves that vents water to atmosphere if either check valve fails, providing a higher level of protection the double check valve assembly does not offer. An air gap, which is a physical separation between the water outlet and the flood rim of a receiving vessel, is the only backflow prevention method that requires no mechanical parts and carries no failure mode, but it cannot be used in a pressurized piped system. The double check assembly sits between the single check valve and the RPZ on the hazard scale, making it the standard choice for irrigation systems connected to a potable supply where fertilizer injection or other low-hazard additives are not present. ### Double check valve assembly inspection and testing

Double check valve assembly inspection is a periodic procedure. A licensed tester checks both check valves for tight closure, verifies the shutoff valves operate freely, and confirms no visible corrosion or leakage at the body or test cocks. Testing goes further: the tester attaches a differential pressure gauge kit to the test cocks and measures the pressure differential held by each check valve independently. A check valve that fails to hold the minimum differential pressure must be repaired or the assembly replaced before the system returns to service. Debris is the most common cause of failure. Even a small particle lodged on the rubber disc prevents full closure of the check valve seat. Freeze damage is a secondary cause in climates where the assembly is installed above grade without insulation, because ice expansion can crack the brass body or distort the check valve seats. Homeowners often schedule [winterization and blowout](https://local-irrigation.mintlify.site/winterization-and-blowout) services together with backflow device testing in regions subject to freezing temperatures.

### Common problems and signs of failure

Double check valve assembly problems fall into three recognizable patterns. First, a leaking or dripping assembly at the test cock ports or body joints signals a failed seat, a cracked body or a loose fitting that needs immediate attention. Second, a drop in system pressure downstream of the assembly, without any change in demand, points to a check valve that is not opening fully, often because a spring has weakened or debris is blocking the disc. Third, a failed annual test, where the tester records a differential pressure below the minimum threshold on either check valve, means the assembly is no longer providing reliable backflow protection even if no visible leak is present. An [impact sprinkler](https://local-irrigation.mintlify.site/impact-sprinkler) zone that shows uneven pressure across heads can sometimes trace back to a partially blocked check valve in the assembly rather than a zone valve or head problem. Replacing internal components, specifically the rubber disc and spring on the failing check valve, restores function without replacing the entire assembly body in most cases. ### Where double check valve assemblies apply

Double check valve assemblies are the standard backflow prevention device for residential and commercial irrigation systems connected to a municipal potable water supply, for fire sprinkler systems in low-hazard classifications and for boiler makeup water connections where the fluid in the system is water only. In irrigation, the assembly installs at the point of connection between the potable main and the irrigation supply line, upstream of any zone valves, pressure regulators or filters. Horizontal installation is the most common orientation for double check assemblies, though some body designs are rated for vertical installation when horizontal space is limited. The double check assembly is not acceptable for connections where chemicals, fertilizers or reclaimed water enter the irrigation line; those applications require an RPZ assembly or an air gap to meet the higher hazard classification. Backflow prevention requirements can vary and should be confirmed with the local building authority.
