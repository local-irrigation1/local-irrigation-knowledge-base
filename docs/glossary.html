Glossary — Local Irrigation

Promotional, educational information about Local Irrigation. This site is not operated by Local Irrigation.

  • Seasonal Sprinkler Adjustment: Seasonal sprinkler adjustment, called seasonal sprinkler tuning, is the process of modifying a sprinkler system's controller schedule, nozzle settings, zone runtimes and pressure regulation to match the water demands of the current season, covering services from spring startup checks and summer peak-heat programming to fall drawdown and pre-winter reductions.
  • Winterization and Blowout: Winterization and blowout, called sprinkler blowout or irrigation winterization, is the process of draining and purging water from a sprinkler system's pipes, valves, heads, and backflow device using compressed air.
  • System Calibration: System calibration, called Equipment Calibration, is the adjustment of a sprinkler system's pressure, flow rate, nozzle alignment, and zone timing so that every part of the landscape receives water at the rate and coverage the design specifies.
  • Irrigation Controller Repair: Irrigation controller repair is the diagnosis and fixing of malfunctioning electronic irrigation timers and controllers.
  • Smart Irrigation Controller: A smart irrigation controller, called an Automatic Irrigation Controller, is an electronic device that automatically adjusts watering schedules based on real-time data from weather feeds, soil sensors, and local evapotranspiration rates.
  • WiFi Sprinkler Controller: A WiFi sprinkler controller, called a smart sprinkler controller, is an electronic device that automates and remotely controls sprinkler zone valves through a 2.4 GHz WiFi connection.
  • Weather-Based Controllers: Weather-based controllers, called ET controllers or smart irrigation controllers, are automated irrigation devices that adjust watering schedules in real time based on local weather data including temperature, humidity, wind speed, solar radiation and rainfall, replacing the fixed timer logic of a basic irrigation controller with a system that responds to what the landscape actually needs.
  • Hunter Irrigation Controller: Hunter Irrigation Controller is an electronic device that automates watering schedules for residential and commercial sprinkler systems, managing zone timing, program sequencing, run duration, power distribution and connectivity across 4 to 48 stations from a single wall-mounted or pedestal-mounted unit.
  • Rain Bird Irrigation Controller: Rain Bird Irrigation Controller is an electronic device that automates watering schedules for residential and commercial sprinkler systems, managing station runtimes, programming multiple daily schedules, controlling 4 to 48 zones, operating on 24VAC power and housed in either indoor or outdoor enclosures depending on the installation.
  • Master Valve: A master valve, called a Main Valve or System Isolation Valve, is an irrigation valve installed at the point where the main water supply enters a sprinkler system.
  • Zone Valve Solenoid: A zone valve solenoid, called an irrigation solenoid, is an electromechanical valve component that actuates a zone valve to start or stop water flow through a specific section of a sprinkler system, controlling each irrigation zone independently by converting an electrical signal from the controller into mechanical movement.
  • Automatic Shut-Off Valves: Automatic shut-off valves, called Automatic Safety Valves, are a type of valve that stops water flow on its own the moment a leak, burst, or pressure fault is detected in a sprinkler or irrigation system, covering supply lines, zone valves, lateral pipes and the main feed without any manual action from the homeowner.
  • Anti-siphon Valve: An anti-siphon valve, called a backflow prevention valve, is a one-way flow control device installed on irrigation systems.
  • Pressure Regulating Valve: A pressure regulating valve, called a Pressure Control Valve or PRV, is a type of control valve that maintains consistent downstream water pressure in a sprinkler system by automatically reducing and stabilizing the flow from the supply line, protecting heads, lateral pipes, fittings and the controller from pressure spikes that cause misting, uneven coverage and premature wear.
  • Reduced Pressure Zone Assembly: A reduced pressure zone assembly, called an RPZ valve, is a mechanical backflow prevention device that uses a reduced pressure zone between two independent check valves to stop contaminated water from flowing backward into a potable water supply, protecting drinking water from irrigation chemicals, fertilizers, pesticides and other hazardous substances that enter the system through cross-connections.
  • Double Check Valve Assembly: A double check valve assembly, called a double check valve or double check assembly, is a type of backflow preventer consisting of two independent spring-loaded check valves arranged in series inside a single valve body, with test cocks and shutoff valves at each end, designed to stop contaminated water from flowing backward into a potable water supply.
  • Spray Sprinkler Head: A spray sprinkler head, called a spray head or fixed spray nozzle, distributes water in a fixed fan or circular pattern across a defined zone of lawn or landscape.
  • Drip Emitter: A drip emitter, called a dripline emitter, is an irrigation component that delivers water directly to the root zone of plants at a controlled, low-volume rate, drop by drop, rather than broadcasting water across a wide area the way a spray head or rotor does.
  • Drip Line: Drip line, called emitter hose or inline emitter tubing, is a type of drip irrigation tubing with factory-installed emitters built directly into the tubing wall at fixed intervals, designed to deliver water slowly and precisely at root level rather than broadcasting it through the air.
  • PVC Pipes: PVC pipes, called Plastic Pipes, are rigid polyvinyl chloride conduits used to carry water through irrigation systems, residential plumbing, and drainage networks, covering supply lines, lateral lines, mainlines, zone feeds and underground distribution runs.
  • Polyethylene Pipe: Polyethylene pipe, commonly called PE pipe or poly pipe, is a flexible plastic pipe used to carry water through irrigation and sprinkler systems.
  • Leakage Detection: Leakage detection, called Leak Locating, is the systematic process of locating unintended fluid escapes in a pressurized system, covering pressure testing, acoustic listening, flow metering, thermal imaging and sensor-based monitoring across residential and commercial irrigation networks.
  • Broken Sprinkler Head: A broken sprinkler head, called a defective sprinkler head, is a sprinkler system component that is physically damaged, cracked, missing its cap, or otherwise fails to distribute water in the correct pattern across the intended coverage zone.
  • Clogged Nozzles: Clogged nozzles, called blocked nozzles, are a sprinkler system condition where debris, mineral deposits, or particulates obstruct the water outlet of a sprinkler nozzle, reducing or stopping flow through that outlet entirely.
  • Low Sprinkler Water Pressure: Low sprinkler water pressure is a water distribution issue.
  • High Sprinkler Water Pressure: High sprinkler water pressure, called over-pressurization, occurs when water pressure inside irrigation lines exceeds the designed operating range.
  • Uneven Water Distribution: Uneven water distribution, called inconsistent watering, occurs when an irrigation system delivers different volumes of water across a coverage area.
  • Wet Spots: Wet spots, called soggy patches, are visible areas of excessive surface water or waterlogged soil.
  • Frozen Pipes: Frozen pipes, called ice-blocked pipes, are a plumbing problem in which standing water inside a pipe solidifies into ice, blocking flow and building dangerous pressure upstream of the blockage.
  • Electrical Malfunctions: Electrical malfunctions, called power issues or electrical failures, are a type of malfunction in which electrical current or components fail to function correctly, disrupting the signals, voltages and currents that control sprinkler valves, timers, zone wiring and sensor circuits across an irrigation system.
  • Sprinkler Controller Malfunctions: Sprinkler controller malfunctions, called sprinkler controller errors, are unintended operational failures in the programmable timer or control unit.
  • Controller Programming Error: A controller programming error, called an Irrigation Controller Fault, is a misconfiguration in a sprinkler controller's schedule or logic settings.
  • Backflow Prevention Regulations: Backflow Prevention Regulations, called Water Backflow Rules, are a type of Regulatory Requirements that mandate the installation, testing and maintenance of approved backflow prevention devices on water supply connections where a cross-connection risk exists, covering residential irrigation systems, commercial plumbing, fire suppression lines and any other point where non-potable water could reverse into the public supply.
  • Municipal Irrigation Codes: Municipal irrigation codes, called City Water Codes, are official local government rules that detail the requirements for irrigation system design, installation and operation within a defined legal jurisdiction.
  • Local Plumbing Codes: Local plumbing codes, called Plumbing Regulations or Municipal Plumbing Ordinances, are legally binding rules governing how plumbing and irrigation systems are designed, installed, tested, and maintained within a specific city or county.
  • Licensed Sprinkler Contractor: A licensed sprinkler contractor is typically a legally authorized trade professional who installs, repairs, inspects, and maintains irrigation systems.
  • Insured Sprinkler Contractor: An insured sprinkler contractor, called a bonded sprinkler contractor, is a type of licensed sprinkler contractor who carries active general liability insurance and workers compensation coverage alongside a valid state contractor license.
  • Water Conservation Measures: Water conservation measures, called water saving strategies, are actions taken to reduce water usage and waste across irrigation systems, landscapes, and municipal water supplies.
  • Wiring Diagrams: Wiring diagrams, called electrical schematics, are technical drawings that map the connections, components and current paths inside an electrical system using standardized symbols, labeled lines, wire gauge callouts, voltage references and connection points so a technician can trace every circuit without touching a single wire.
  • Irrigation System Repair: Irrigation system repair, called Irrigation Repair, is the diagnosis and restoration of malfunctioning watering systems.
  • Sprinkler Controller Repair: Sprinkler controller repair, called sprinkler timer repair, is the diagnostic and corrective service for malfunctioning irrigation controllers.
  • Irrigation Valve Repair: Irrigation valve repair, called Irrigation Control Valve Repair, is the diagnosis and correction of faults in zone control valves.
  • Backflow Prevention: Backflow prevention, called a backflow valve or cross-connection control, is a set of measures and mechanical assemblies that stop non-potable water from reversing into a potable water supply line.
  • Sprinkler Heads: Sprinkler heads, called spray heads, are mechanical devices attached to irrigation pipes that distribute water across a lawn or landscape in a controlled pattern.
  • Drip Irrigation Components: Drip irrigation components, called micro-irrigation components, are the individual parts required to assemble, extend or repair a water-efficient drip irrigation system, including emitters, drip tubing, connectors, fittings, pressure regulators, filters and end caps.
