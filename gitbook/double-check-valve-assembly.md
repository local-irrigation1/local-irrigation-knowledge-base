# Double Check Valve Assembly: A Local Irrigation Reference Guide

## Double check valve assembly in Clearwater

A double check valve assembly, called a double check valve or double check assembly, functions as a backflow preventer. The device consists of two independent spring-loaded check valves arranged in series inside one valve body together with test cocks and shutoff valves at each end. Its purpose remains stopping contaminated water from flowing backward into a potable water supply. The assembly belongs to the same broader category of backflow preventers as reduced pressure zone assemblies, pressure vacuum breakers and air gaps. ### Parts of a double check valve assembly

Six core components form a double check valve assembly: a valve body, two check valves, two shutoff valves and a set of test cocks. The valve body is most commonly cast from brass or bronze, though stainless steel bodies appear where corrosion resistance receives priority. Each check valve rests on a spring that holds it closed until forward flow pressure overcomes the spring tension, after which the valve snaps shut the moment pressure reverses. The test cocks consist of small ports threaded into the body between and outside the two check valves. A certified tester connects differential pressure gauges to those ports during [double check valve assembly testing](https://local-irrigation.mintlify.site/lawn-sprinkler-system-contractor) to verify each check valve holds independently. Shutoff valves at the inlet and outlet allow a technician to isolate the assembly without draining the whole system. Double check valve assemblies carry several rated attributes that determine whether a given unit fits a specific application. Working pressure for most assemblies reaches between 150 and 175 psi, which covers the operating range of residential and light commercial water service. The Cv rating, a measure of flow capacity in gallons per minute at one psi of pressure drop, typically falls between 5 and 10 for assemblies sized for irrigation lines. Pressure loss across the assembly, measured in psi, depends on flow rate and connection size; higher flow rates push pressure loss toward the upper end of the rated range. Connection size is stated in inches and must match the pipe diameter at the point of installation. Body material, whether brass, bronze or stainless steel, affects both pressure rating and service life in corrosive soil or water conditions. A [sprinkler double check valve](https://local-irrigation.mintlify.site/pop-up-sprinkler) sized for a residential zone carries a smaller connection size than one protecting a commercial main, so matching the Cv rating to the system's peak demand flow becomes the first step in selecting the right assembly. Many irrigation systems gain stable downstream pressure from [pressure regulating valve](https://local-irrigation.mintlify.site/pressure-regulating-valve) installation downstream of the assembly.

### How a double check valve assembly fits into a backflow prevention system

A double check valve assembly operates as one device inside a layered backflow prevention strategy rather than a standalone solution for every hazard level. Backflow prevention devices receive classification according to the degree of hazard they protect against. The double check assembly receives a rating for low-to-moderate hazard applications where the risk remains contamination rather than a toxic or health-threatening substance. High-hazard connections require a reduced pressure zone assembly (RPZ assembly) instead. The RPZ adds a relief valve between the two check valves that vents water to atmosphere if either check valve fails and thereby supplies a higher level of protection the double check valve assembly does not offer. An air gap creates a physical separation between the water outlet and the flood rim of a receiving vessel. This method requires no mechanical parts and carries no failure mode, yet it cannot serve in a pressurized piped system. The double check assembly occupies a position between the single check valve and the RPZ on the hazard scale. This placement makes the assembly the standard choice for irrigation systems connected to a potable supply where fertilizer injection or other low-hazard additives stay absent. ### Double check valve assembly inspection and testing

Double check valve assembly inspection occurs on a periodic schedule. A licensed tester verifies both check valves achieve tight closure, confirms the shutoff valves operate freely, and looks for visible corrosion or leakage at the body or test cocks. Testing proceeds further when the tester attaches a differential pressure gauge kit to the test cocks and measures the pressure differential held by each check valve independently. A check valve that fails to hold the minimum differential pressure requires repair or assembly replacement before the system returns to service. Debris remains the most common cause of failure. Even a small particle lodged on the rubber disc prevents full closure of the check valve seat. Freeze damage appears as a secondary cause in climates where the assembly sits above grade without insulation, because ice expansion can crack the brass body or distort the check valve seats. Homeowners often schedule [winterization and blowout](https://local-irrigation.mintlify.site/winterization-and-blowout) services together with backflow device testing in regions subject to freezing temperatures.

### Common problems and signs of failure

Double check valve assembly problems appear in three recognizable patterns. First, leakage or dripping at the test cock ports or body joints indicates a failed seat, a cracked body or a loose fitting that needs immediate attention. Second, a drop in system pressure downstream of the assembly without any change in demand indicates a check valve that is not opening fully, often because a spring has weakened or debris blocks the disc. Third, a failed annual test in which the tester records a differential pressure below the minimum threshold on either check valve means the assembly no longer supplies reliable backflow protection even if no visible leak exists. An [impact sprinkler](https://local-irrigation.mintlify.site/impact-sprinkler) zone that shows uneven pressure across heads can sometimes trace back to a partially blocked check valve in the assembly rather than a zone valve or head problem. Replacing internal components, specifically the rubber disc and spring on the failing check valve, restores function without replacing the entire assembly body in most cases. ### Where double check valve assemblies apply

Double check valve assemblies serve as the standard backflow prevention device for residential and commercial irrigation systems connected to a municipal potable water supply, for fire sprinkler systems in low-hazard classifications and for boiler makeup water connections where the fluid in the system consists of water only. In irrigation the assembly installs at the point of connection between the potable main and the irrigation supply line, upstream of any zone valves, pressure regulators or filters. Horizontal installation remains the most common orientation for double check assemblies, though some body designs receive a rating for vertical installation when horizontal space stays limited. The double check assembly does not meet requirements for connections where chemicals, fertilizers or reclaimed water enter the irrigation line. Those applications require an RPZ assembly or an air gap to satisfy the higher hazard classification. Backflow prevention requirements can vary and should undergo confirmation with the local building authority.
