## Weather-based controllers in Clearwater

Weather-based controllers, called ET controllers or smart irrigation controllers, are automated irrigation devices that adjust watering schedules in real time based on local weather data including temperature, humidity, wind speed, solar radiation and rainfall, replacing the fixed timer logic of a basic irrigation controller with a system that responds to what the landscape actually needs. ### Parts and scope of a weather-based controller

Weather-based controllers consist of a controller unit, a control panel with wiring terminals, communication modules and at least one rain sensor, all working together to translate incoming weather signals into zone-by-zone run-time adjustments. The controller unit is the central processor. It receives data, applies an evapotranspiration (ET) calculation and writes a new schedule without manual input. Rain sensors attach to the system and measure precipitation in millimeters per hour, cutting off a scheduled cycle the moment rainfall crosses the programmed sensitivity threshold. Communication modules connect the controller to a local weather station or a cloud-based weather network, and wiring terminals tie each irrigation zone into the control panel so adjustments reach every head in the field. ### Measurable attributes of weather-based controllers

Weather-based controllers carry several measurable attributes that determine how well they fit a given property. The number of zones supported typically ranges from 4 to 16 zones, covering services from a small residential lawn to a mid-size commercial landscape. Rain sensor sensitivity is adjustable and is measured in millimeters per hour, letting a technician set the exact precipitation rate at which the controller suspends irrigation. Connectivity range for wireless communication modules varies by model and site conditions, and the power source is 24V AC, the same standard used across most residential and light-commercial irrigation systems. Programming capability is expressed in degrees of schedule granularity. The controller can be set to respond to temperature swings of a defined number of degrees before recalculating run times. Water savings from a properly calibrated weather-based controller installation reach measurable percentages compared to a fixed-schedule timer, because the system never runs a zone when recent rainfall or a forecasted storm already covers the water budget. A [smart irrigation controller](https://local-irrigation.mintlify.site/smart-irrigation-controller) shares most of these attributes and is often used as a synonym for the ET controller class.

### How weather-based controllers relate to the broader irrigation system

Weather-based controllers are a component within a complete sprinkler system, serviced by sprinkler repair professionals and used in sprinkler systems of all sizes. The controller sits upstream of every zone valve. Its output signal opens and closes valves, so a misconfigured controller affects every zone simultaneously. Sprinkler repair work often begins at the controller when a homeowner reports that zones are running at the wrong time or not at all, because the controller is the single point that governs the whole system. ### Weather-based controllers vs. Time-based and manual controllers

A time-based controller runs every zone on a fixed schedule regardless of rainfall, temperature, or soil moisture. It waters on Tuesday and Saturday whether the lawn needs it or not. A manual sprinkler timer requires a person to physically change the schedule each time conditions shift, which rarely happens in practice. Manual controllers offer no automation at all. They depend entirely on the operator's attention. Weather-based controllers replace all of that fixed logic with a live data feed, so the schedule changes automatically when a cold front drops temperatures or a rain event saturates the soil. The practical result is that a property running a weather sensor controller avoids both overwatering during wet weeks and underwatering during heat events. Two failure modes that a basic irrigation controller cannot address on its own.

### Common problems and signs a weather-based controller needs service

Weather-based controllers show recognizable failure signs that distinguish them from zone-valve or head problems. Zones running during or immediately after rain indicate the rain sensor has lost its connection to the controller unit, or the sensor's sensitivity threshold is set too high for local rainfall rates. Some zones never run. When this occurs despite dry conditions, it points to a communication module that has lost its weather data feed, leaving the controller without the ET input it needs to calculate run times. Wiring terminal corrosion causes intermittent zone failures that appear random but trace back to a single loose or oxidized connection at the control panel. A controller that holds the correct schedule on screen but fails to open valves on time usually has a 24V AC power supply issue rather than a programming fault. Sprinkler inspection identifies which failure mode is active before any parts are replaced. This saves time on properties with 8 or more zones, where a misdiagnosis means testing every terminal individually.

### Where weather-based controllers apply in Clearwater

Clearwater's climate suits weather-based controllers well. Summer afternoon thunderstorms can deliver an inch of rain in under an hour, then give way to two weeks of dry heat. A fixed-schedule timer has no way to account for that variability, so properties running time-based controllers routinely overwater in June and underwater in October. ET controllers read local conditions continuously and recalculate zone run times to match actual evapotranspiration rates, which in Clearwater shift significantly between the wet season and the dry season. Weather-based controllers can be installed on new construction or as a retrofit to an existing system. The wiring terminals and 24V AC power source are compatible with most sprinkler systems already in the ground, so a retrofit typically requires no new pipe work. Only a controller swap and sensor placement.
